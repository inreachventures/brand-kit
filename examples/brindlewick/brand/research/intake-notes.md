# Intake notes

Interview on 2026-11-20 with Maya Okafor, co-founder and CEO. Tom Reyes (CTO) did not take part;
his writing sample came through Maya.

## The company
In Maya's words: shops use Brindlewick "to take repair bookings online, quote jobs, text the
customer when the bike's ready, and reorder parts from distributors in one place."

- Customers: independent bike repair shops.
- Business model: a monthly subscription per shop.
- Markets: 140 paying shops in the UK and Ireland; about 20 in the Netherlands since spring 2026.
- Size and age: nine people, fourteen months old, seed stage.
- Name: Brindlewick, settled.

## Customers and other audiences
- **Who pays and who uses:** nearly always the same person, the owner-mechanic of a shop with one
  to six people. "In the workshop with greasy hands, answer the phone mid-repair, do the admin at
  nine at night."
- **Best customer:** no specific story yet. The typical customer ran everything off a paper diary
  and WhatsApp before.
- **Why shops leave or do not sign up:** no clear pattern yet. Maya's guess is that shops stay on
  paper "because changing feels like more admin". Unverified.
- **Other audiences:** bike parts distributors who want to be listed in the parts catalogue; people
  Brindlewick hires (engineers and support staff who like bikes).
- **How Maya would like a shop to describe Brindlewick:** "It's made by someone who's actually run
  a shop." She was unsure about this.

## Stage and the next twelve months
- Seed, with paying customers and growing.
- Next twelve months: grow in the Netherlands; hire more engineers and support people. A further
  fundraise is likely at some point, with no date.
- What the brand must help with first: winning shops. Hiring second.

## Competitors and alternatives
- **ShopLedger:** generic retail till software with a repairs add-on. Corporate, blue, says
  "all-in-one".
- **SpokeDesk:** US repair-booking app. Slick, startup purple, says "seamless" a lot.
- **Workshop Pro:** old desktop software with beige screenshots and loyal older users.
- **Paper diary and WhatsApp:** what most shops actually use.
- **Admired:** no brand named. Maya likes old workshop materials: steel, enamel paint, chalk on a
  blackboard.
- **What annoys Maya:** "seamless". "Nothing in a workshop is seamless."

## Constraints
- **Budget:** small. A designer for a logo symbol is possible later.
- **Deadlines:** none fixed.
- **Claims not to make:** individual shop revenue; distributor margins. No regulated sector.
- **Languages and scripts:** English. Dutch shops use the product in English for now; translation
  is undecided. Latin script only.
- **Dislikes:** "disruptive", startup purple, anything that looks like a bank.
- **Likes:** steel, enamel paint, chalk on a blackboard. "Warm, not twee."
