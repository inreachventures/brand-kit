# Competitor analysis

2026-11-20. Sources: founder input only.

**Limited research.** This analysis was done without web access, and Maya had no competitor copy
or screenshots to hand. Everything below comes from her descriptions in the intake interview. No
competitor headline is quoted, because none was available to quote, and no colour is given as a
hex value, because none could be read from a page. Treat it as a map of how the founder sees the
market, and check it against the competitors' own sites before relying on any detail.

## Scope
- **ShopLedger** (adjacent): retail till software with a repairs add-on. A shop that already owns
  it for the till may never look further.
- **SpokeDesk** (direct): a US repair-booking app. Same job as Brindlewick's bookings, in a
  different market.
- **Workshop Pro** (direct, older generation): desktop workshop software that some shops already
  run, with loyal older users.
- **Paper diary and WhatsApp** (status quo): what most shops use, and what most Brindlewick
  customers switched from.

No admired brand is in the set; Maya did not name one.

## Category clichés

### Words and claims
- **"All-in-one"**: ShopLedger, and Brindlewick's own homepage today.
- **"Seamless"**: SpokeDesk, and Brindlewick's own homepage today.
- **"Modern"** (as in "modern bike shops"): Brindlewick's own homepage. Treat it as a cliché
  because it defines the customer by contrast with Workshop Pro rather than by what they do.
- **"Cutting-edge", "revolutionise", "leverage"**: Brindlewick's own sales line. Not seen in
  competitors only because their copy was not available; these are general software clichés.

With so little competitor copy, these counts are thin. The two words Maya named, "all-in-one" and
"seamless", are the ones she hears most, which is the useful test.

### Colours and imagery
- **Blue** (ShopLedger): reads as retail and finance software. Maya rules out anything that looks
  like a bank.
- **Purple** (SpokeDesk): the startup software colour. Ruled out by Maya.
- **Beige and grey screenshots** (Workshop Pro): reads as old desktop software.
- **Product screenshots** as the main image: likely across all three software competitors, not
  confirmed.

### Tone
Corporate (ShopLedger) or slick startup (SpokeDesk). Neither, as Maya describes them, sounds like
someone who has worked in a shop.

## Gaps
- **A voice from the workshop.** Nobody in the set sounds like a mechanic. Evidence: Maya's
  descriptions; her own posts are the opposite of "slick". Why it may be empty: software
  companies are rarely founded by mechanics.
- **The paper diary as the real competitor.** Most shops use paper and WhatsApp (Maya), but the
  software competitors, as described, compete with each other on features. Why it may be empty:
  "better than paper" sounds like a small claim to a software company.
- **Workshop colours.** Nobody uses warm, material colours such as enamel paint, steel or chalk on a
  blackboard. The category sits in blue, purple and beige.
- **A founder who ran a shop.** A claim nobody else in the set can make, as far as we know.
- **Parts ordering as a reason to switch.** Not mentioned for any competitor. Unknown whether
  they offer it; it may be a gap or may just be missing from Maya's descriptions.

## For stage 2 to test

- Does a plain, workshop voice give up the larger shops that expect a "proper" software company?
