# Communication strategy

Where Brindlewick talks, how often, about what, and who signs it off, from December 2026 to the
review on 20 February 2027 and then for the rest of 2027 if the review keeps it.

## Goals
1. **Win shops.** More owner-mechanics hear about Brindlewick from someone they trust, and sign
   up. Most shops find Brindlewick through other shops, so communication should give existing
   customers something worth passing on and give new ones a reason to believe "made by someone
   who's actually run a shop". This includes the Netherlands, with the same messages for now.
2. **Help hiring.** Engineers and support people who like bikes see how the team thinks before
   they apply. Brindlewick plans to hire in the next twelve months.

When they compete for the same hour, shops come first.

## Audiences
- **Shop owners:** read Maya's posts and the monthly email, and tell another shop. See
  `strategy/audience-shop-owners.md`.
- **Hires:** read Tom's posts and apply. See `strategy/audience-hires.md`.
- **Distributors:** not addressed by this plan's channels; Tom deals with them directly. See
  `strategy/audience-distributors.md`.

## What we say
The core message, the messages for each audience and the phrases are in
`strategy/messaging-framework.md`. Themes to put first in this period:
- Maya's six years at the stand, through specific repairs and customers, not as a biography.
- The paper diary as the comparison: what a booking, an "is it ready?" call and a parts order cost
  a shop on paper.
- How the product is built, from Tom: one decision at a time, with the reason.

## Owned channels

| Channel | Audience | Purpose | Owner | Formats |
|---------|----------|---------|-------|---------|

| Tom's LinkedIn | Hires, and shop owners curious about the product | Show how the product is built and how the team thinks | Tom | Longer posts on one problem and its solution |

## Cadence: the committed minimum

### By channel

| Channel | Minimum | Fixed day or time | Owner |
|---------|---------|-------------------|-------|
| Maya's LinkedIn | Two posts a month (weekly when possible) | none | Maya |
| Tom's LinkedIn | Two posts a month (weekly when possible) | none | Tom |
| Monthly email to shops | One a month | First Tuesday of the month | Maya |
| Instagram | No minimum: a bonus | none | Whoever is free |
| Website | Updated when the product or the numbers change | none | Maya |

### By person

| Person | Commitment | Channels | Hours per week |
|--------|------------|----------|----------------|
| Maya Okafor | Two LinkedIn posts a month at least; the monthly email | LinkedIn, email, website | About 1, plus 2 hours a month for the email |
| Tom Reyes | Two LinkedIn posts a month at least; reviews the monthly email | LinkedIn | About 2 |

The aim of a post a week each is what Maya called realistic. The minimum is what survives a bad
month. Anything above it is a bonus, not a missed target.

## Where topics come from
- **Maya:** repairs and customers she hears about from shops; questions shops ask in onboarding
  and support; things she disagrees with in how software is sold to shops.
- **Tom:** product changes as they ship; problems such as distributors' four names for one brake
  pad; decisions the team argued about.
- **Support:** the most common question of the month goes in the topic bank and is a candidate for
  the email's tip.
- **Where it lives:** a document in the shared drive. Anyone can add a line; Maya reads it when
  she plans the email.

## Reuse
- A long post of Tom's becomes the "what changed" section of the next email, cut to a paragraph by
  Maya.
- A shop story in the email becomes an Instagram post if the shop agrees, and can be one of
  Maya's LinkedIn posts.
- The most useful support answers become a short page on the website over time.
Maya does the adapting; it is part of her two hours a month on the email.

## Approval and rules

### Who approves what

| Content | Written by | Approved by | Turnaround |
|---------|------------|-------------|------------|
| Maya's and Tom's own LinkedIn posts | the author | no approval needed | none |
| Anything that names or shows a shop | anyone | that shop, in writing | before it goes out |
| Monthly email to shops | Maya | Tom, for product accuracy | one working day |
| Website copy | Maya | Tom, for product pages | one working day |
| Instagram posts | anyone | no approval needed, once the shop has agreed | none |

### Never say in public
- An individual shop's revenue, ever.
- Distributor margins, ever.
- Order volumes through the catalogue.
- The price, until Maya decides to publish it.
- The number of distributors, until Tom confirms it.
- A shop's name, photo or words without that shop's permission.
- Any number of hours or calls saved: none has been measured.
See the channel calibration in `strategy/messaging-framework.md`.

### Replies and difficult moments
- Maya answers comments on her posts, Tom on his. [Open: how quickly.]
- Product complaints in public get a short reply from the author and go to support, who follow up
  directly.
- [Open: who handles press enquiries. Maya, unless she says otherwise.]
- If something serious happens in public, Maya decides what Brindlewick says and when.

## Measurement

| Goal | Measure | Source | Checked |
|------|---------|--------|---------|
| Win shops | Shops at signup who say they heard of Brindlewick from a post, the email or another shop | "How did you hear about us?" at signup (to be added) | monthly |
| Win shops | Replies to the monthly email | the email inbox | monthly |
| Win shops | Shops that recommend Brindlewick to another shop | signup question, support notes | quarterly |
| Help hiring | Applicants who found Brindlewick through Tom's or Maya's posts | "Where did you see this job?" on applications (to be added) | per role |
| Both | Posts published against the minimum | the team's own count | monthly |

Early signals: email replies, posts published, comments from shop owners. Results: signups and
applicants who name a post or another shop.

## Review
On 20 February 2027, Maya and Tom review the first 90 days: posts against the minimum, what the
signup and application questions show, and whether Instagram earns a fixed cadence or is dropped.
They set the minimums for the next period from what actually happened.
