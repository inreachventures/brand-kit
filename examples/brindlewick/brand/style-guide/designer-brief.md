# Logo brief: Brindlewick

Written on 2026-11-20 for a future commission. Maya to review before sending.

## The company
Brindlewick makes booking and parts-ordering software for independent bike repair shops. Shops
use it to take repair bookings online, quote jobs, text customers when the bike is ready, and
reorder parts from distributors in one place. It is a seed-stage company of nine people, fourteen
months old, used by 140 shops in the UK and Ireland and about 20 in the Netherlands. The CEO, Maya
Okafor, ran a bike repair shop in Leeds for six years.

## Positioning
"For the owner-mechanics of independent bike repair shops, Brindlewick is the workshop software
that fits how a repair shop actually runs, from the booking to the parts order, because it was
built by a mechanic who ran a shop in Leeds for six years and is used by 140 shops in the UK and
Ireland."

It gives up chains and sales-led shops, the feature-list fight, and any claim to be the most
advanced. Tagline: "Workshop software, made by a mechanic."

## Audiences
- Shop owners: owner-mechanics of one-to-six-person shops; the main audience. They trust other
  mechanics, not software companies.
- Parts distributors who list their catalogues in Brindlewick.
- Engineers and support staff who like bikes, for hiring.

## Personality
Brindlewick sounds like a mechanic talking to another mechanic across the counter. Its voice
principles: name the part, not the category; plain, but warm; start at the bench; confident,
never loud; explain the why, in a sentence. For a designer that means: made, not decorated;
sturdy rather than sleek; warm, not twee.

It looks like a mechanic at the counter, a job card, an old enamel sign. It does not look like a
software salesperson, a brochure or a bank.

## Existing identity
- Colours: enamel green #2B5A36 (primary), signal orange #E0662B (accent), workshop black
  #1A2119 (ink), chalk white #F7F4EC (paper); warm greys #ECE8DE, #CFC9BA, #6B675C, #45423B.
- Fonts: IBM Plex Serif Bold for headings and the current wordmark; IBM Plex Sans for text; IBM
  Plex Mono for part numbers.
- The current wordmark (the name in Plex Serif Bold) and the "B" tile in `assets/logos/`. The
  symbol should sit with the wordmark; the wordmark may be refined but the name stays
  "Brindlewick".
- Materials Maya likes: steel, enamel paint, chalk on a blackboard.

## The category
Competitors, as the founder describes them: ShopLedger is corporate and blue; SpokeDesk is slick
startup purple; Workshop Pro looks like old beige desktop software. Their logos were not reviewed
for this brief. Avoid blue, purple and beige; avoid anything that looks like a bank or a generic
app icon. Avoid the obvious bicycle clichés too (a wheel, a chainring, a bike silhouette), unless
the designer can make one that no other bike business already has.

## What we need
- A symbol that works with the wordmark.
- Horizontal and stacked versions of the full logo.
- Full colour, black and white versions.
- A square version legible at 16 px, for the favicon and app icon.
- SVG and PNG files, and the source files.
- Backgrounds for the social banners and the video-call background, in place of the plain green
  field the build produces. Maya turned down the generated circle pattern as "like bubbles, not a
  workshop". Something closer to steel or enamel, or plain.

## Where it will be used
Website, the product (a web app used on phones and tablets in workshops), social avatars,
documents and quotes sent to shop customers, email signatures, and possibly stickers or signs for
customer shops.
