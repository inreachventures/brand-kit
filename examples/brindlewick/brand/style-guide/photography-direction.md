# Photography and imagery

Photographs show real shops and real repairs, because the brand rests on "made by someone who's
actually run a shop". Maya answered the subject, consent and AI questions; the rest of this file is
proposed defaults she accepted (see `decisions.md`).

## Principles
- **Real shops, real mess.** Maya: "Shops, mostly. Real ones, real mess. Some team."
- **Start at the bench.** Lead with hands, tools and bikes at work, the way the copy starts with a
  bike or a customer. A proposed default.
- **Warm, not twee.** Maya's phrase for the whole brand. In photographs: natural workshop light,
  no vintage filters, no props. A proposed default.

## Subjects
- **Show:** customer shops at work, with their permission; the team, some of the time.
- **Do not show:** customer data on a screen (names, phone numbers, prices on a quote). A proposed
  default.

## Style
- Available light where possible, no heavy grading, no tinting to the brand colours.
- Close and mid shots more than wide ones: the part, the hands, the face at work.
Both are proposed defaults.

## People
- Ask every shop and every person before taking or publishing a photo, every time. Maya: "Case by
  case, always ask." Keep a note of who agreed, for what, and when.
- Name a shop only with its permission (see "What we can say where" in
  `strategy/messaging-framework.md`).

## What we avoid
- Beige or grey software screenshots without context (Workshop Pro's look, from the research).
- Anything purple or corporate blue in the frame where it can be avoided (ruled out by Maya).

## AI-generated images
Not allowed. Maya: "If it isn't a real shop it shouldn't look like one."

## Requirements
- Alt text for every image: what is in it, in plain words ("Mechanic replacing a gear cable on a
  1987 Raleigh"). A proposed default.
- Use only photos Brindlewick took or has a written licence for, and record the shop's permission
  with the file. A proposed default.
