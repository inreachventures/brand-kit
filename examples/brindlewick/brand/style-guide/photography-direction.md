# Photography and imagery

Photographs show real shops and real repairs. A brand built on "made by someone who's actually run
a shop" has to look like it has been in one, so the pictures are the proof, not decoration.

## Principles
- **Real shops, real mess.** Maya: "Real ones, real mess." A tidy bench is fine if the shop is
  tidy; nothing is staged to look like a showroom. This is the visual side of "name the part, not
  the category".
- **Start at the bench.** Lead with hands, tools and bikes at work, the way the copy starts with a
  bike or a customer.
- **Warm, not twee.** Natural workshop light, honest colour. No vintage filters, no Instagram
  nostalgia, no cute props.

## Subjects
- **Show:** customer shops at work (with permission); hands on a bike in the stand; the bench, the
  parts drawer, the job tags; a phone or tablet with Brindlewick in use at the stand; the team,
  some of the time, preferably in shops.
- **Do not show:** stock photographs of cyclists in Lycra on mountain roads; people at desks
  looking at dashboards; anyone pretending to be a mechanic; customer data on a screen (names,
  phone numbers, prices on a quote).

## Style
- Available light where possible, warm white balance. No heavy grading.
- Close and mid shots more than wide ones: the part, the hands, the face at work.
- Leave room for a headline on one side when a photo is meant for a banner.
- The palette appears through what is really there (green paint, steel, orange tags, chalk). Do
  not tint photos green.

## People
- Ask every shop and every person before taking or publishing a photo, every time. Maya: "Case by
  case, always ask." Keep a note of who agreed, for what, and when.
- Name a shop only with its permission (see the channel calibration in
  `strategy/messaging-framework.md`).
- Show the shops as they are: owners and mechanics of any age, gender and background. Do not
  arrange a shop to look more diverse, or less, than it is.

## Illustration and icons
- No illustration for now. The brand has no symbol and should not suggest one with bicycle
  icons or drawn wheels.
- Simple line icons are fine in the product and on the website for features (bookings, texts,
  parts), in ink or enamel green, at one consistent stroke weight.

## What we avoid
- Product screenshots as the main image on every page, which is the category's habit.
- Beige or grey software screenshots without context (Workshop Pro's look).
- Stock images of "happy customers" or cyclists who are not in a shop.
- Anything purple or corporate blue in the frame where it can be avoided.

## AI-generated images
Not allowed. Maya: "If it isn't a real shop it shouldn't look like one."

## Requirements
- JPEG for photographs, at least 2000 px on the long side; PNG for screenshots.
- Alt text for every image: what is in it, in plain words ("Mechanic replacing a gear cable on a
  1987 Raleigh").
- Use only photos Brindlewick took or has a written licence for. Record the shop's permission with
  the file.
- [Open: where approved photos are kept and who looks after them.]
