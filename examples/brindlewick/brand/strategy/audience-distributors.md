# Audience: parts distributors

## Who they are
Bike parts distributors that want their catalogue listed in Brindlewick so that shops order from
them. Which distributors, and how many are listed today, is not known in this document (open
question for Tom). The people Brindlewick deals with there are not yet named: probably a sales or
e-commerce manager. Assumption.

## What they need
- **The job:** get orders from independent repair shops.
- **What gets in the way:** shops searching for a part by a name the distributor does not use.
  Distributors "name the same brake pad four different ways" (Tom's post), so a shop searching
  by name can miss a distributor's listing.
- **What triggers interest:** [Not known yet. Check with Tom: how did the first distributors come
  to be listed?]
- **What success looks like:** orders from shops through the catalogue. Assumption.

## How they find and judge us
- Probably through Tom or Maya directly, and through the shops that ask for them. Assumption.
- Evidence they trust: the number of shops on Brindlewick (140 in the UK and Ireland, about 20 in
  the Netherlands) and, later, order volumes. Order volumes are not public.

## What they are not getting today
A shop on a paper diary orders by phone or on each distributor's own site, one at a time. A
distributor whose part names differ from the shop's search terms is easy to miss.

## What we do not know yet
- How many distributors are listed and which can be named. Check: Tom.
- Who at a distributor decides to list. Check: Tom's notes from the first listings.
- What distributors would pay for, if anything. Not part of this brand work.

## Key insight
To a distributor, Brindlewick is a way into about 160 workshops, and matching on manufacturer part
number means their stock shows up whatever they call it. Never mention their margins in public.
