# Positioning

## Chosen direction
**For the owner-mechanics of independent bike repair shops, Brindlewick is the workshop software
that fits how a repair shop actually runs, from the booking to the parts order, because it was
built by a mechanic who ran a shop in Leeds for six years and is used by 140 shops in the UK and
Ireland.**

In plain words: "It's shop software made by someone who's actually run a shop."

### Why this direction
Maya chose option A because "it's true and it's the thing I'd say across a counter." It is the
one claim in the competitor set that nobody else can make (competitor analysis, gaps). It suits
how she already writes: about specific repairs, from the bench. At seed, with 140 shops and growth
coming from shops that talk to each other, the first job of the brand is to win the next shop
owner, and a mechanic is the person a mechanic trusts.

Most shops still run on a paper diary and WhatsApp, so option B's comparison stays: in copy,
Brindlewick is compared with the paper diary, not with other software.

### What we give up
- Chains, and shops where selling bikes matters more than repairing them. They want a till
  system; ShopLedger suits them better.
- The feature-list fight with ShopLedger and SpokeDesk.
- Any claim to be the most advanced, the cheapest or the biggest.
- Leading with parts ordering. It is a reason to stay, not the headline.
- Speaking first to shops switching from Workshop Pro or ShopLedger. They are welcome but not who
  the copy addresses.

### Proof we have
- Maya Okafor ran a bike repair shop in Leeds for six years (Maya).
- 140 paying shops in the UK and Ireland and about 20 in the Netherlands (company records, Maya).
- The parts search matches on the manufacturer part number first, because distributors name the
  same part differently (Tom's post).
- Still to prove: that shops find it fits their day. No customer quote or named shop yet; Maya
  needs to ask shops for permission.

## Options considered

### Option A: Built by a mechanic
- **Statement:** For the owner-mechanics of independent bike repair shops, Brindlewick is the
  workshop software that fits how a repair shop actually runs, because it was built by a mechanic
  who ran a shop for six years.
- **In plain words:** "It's shop software made by someone who's actually run a shop."
- **Emphasises:** the founder's years in a workshop; software shaped around a repair day.
- **Gives up:** chains and sales-led shops; the feature-list fight; any claim to be the most
  advanced.
- **Proof:** Maya's six years in Leeds; 140 shops.
- **Gap used:** a voice from the workshop; a founder who ran a shop.
- **Risks:** it rests on one person, and thins as the company grows; Dutch shops do not know
  Maya; it needs customer quotes to show the fit is real.
- **Outcome:** chosen, with B's comparison inside it.

### Option B: Off the paper diary
- **Statement:** For small repair shops still running on a paper diary and WhatsApp, Brindlewick
  is the booking diary that takes the phone calls for you: customers book online and get a text
  when the bike is ready.
- **In plain words:** "It stops the phone ringing while you've got a bike in the stand."
- **Emphasises:** fewer interruptions during repairs; evenings back.
- **Gives up:** shops switching from other software; parts ordering; it invites a comparison with
  something free.
- **Proof:** Maya's view that most customers came from paper (not measured). No numbers on calls
  or hours saved.
- **Gap used:** the paper diary as the real competitor.
- **Risks:** "better than paper" can sound small; no numbers yet to back the time saved.
- **Outcome:** combined into the chosen direction as the comparison used in copy.

### Option C: Quote and order in one go
- **Statement:** For repair shops that order parts from several distributors, Brindlewick is the
  workshop system where the quote and the parts order are the same job, because its parts search
  matches the same part across distributors by manufacturer part number.
- **In plain words:** "You quote the job and order the parts from the same screen."
- **Emphasises:** the parts catalogue and the distributors in it.
- **Gives up:** the simplest message; shops that buy from one distributor; it drifts towards the
  "all-in-one" cliché.
- **Proof:** Tom's part-number matching. [Open: the number of distributors.]
- **Gap used:** parts ordering, which no competitor is described as offering.
- **Risks:** needs distributor numbers the company has not confirmed; ties the brand to
  distributors' catalogues.
- **Outcome:** rejected as the lead. Maya: "Parts is Tom's baby but it's not why people sign up."
  It becomes a supporting point.
