# Messaging framework

What Brindlewick says and to whom. It rests on the position chosen in `positioning.md`: workshop
software made by someone who has run a shop, compared with the paper diary rather than with other
software.

## Core message
Brindlewick is workshop software made by a mechanic. It takes the bookings, the "is it ready?"
calls and the parts orders off the bench, so the paper diary can go.

## Tagline
**Workshop software, made by a mechanic.**

Maya chose it on the recommendation that it says what Brindlewick is and the one thing no
competitor can say.

### Candidates considered
- "Booking and parts software for independent bike shops": accurate, but any competitor could use
  it.
- "Fewer phone calls mid-repair": a promise with no number behind it yet.
- "The workshop diary that texts your customers": Maya liked it but it leaves out parts. Kept as
  a supporting message for shop owners.
- "Made by someone who's actually run a shop": Maya's own words, too long for a tagline. Used in
  the elevator pitches.
- "Built at the bench": short, but engineers built the software, so it is not quite true.
- "Admin done before you shut up shop": an outcome nobody has measured.
- Struck before the shortlist for using a category cliché: "The all-in-one workshop platform",
  "Seamless bookings for bike shops".

## Product story
An owner-mechanic has a bike in the stand and the phone rings. It is someone wanting a service
next week, or a customer asking if their bike is ready. They wipe their hands, find the paper
diary, write it in, and go back to the bike. The parts for tomorrow's jobs still need ordering
from two distributors whose websites call the same brake pad different things, so that waits until
nine at night.

Most shops still work this way. The calls and the late ordering come out of the hours the
mechanic would otherwise spend on paid repairs.

With Brindlewick, customers book online, and the mechanic quotes the job against the booking.
When the bike is ready, the customer gets a text. Parts for the job are ordered from the same
place, and the search finds a part across distributors by its manufacturer part number, then by
its name.

Maya Okafor built it after six years running a repair shop in Leeds. 140 shops in the UK and
Ireland use it, and about 20 in the Netherlands, where Brindlewick started in spring 2026.

## Elevator pitches

### About 10 seconds
Brindlewick is booking and parts software for independent bike shops, made by a mechanic who ran a
shop for six years. 140 UK and Irish shops use it.

### About 30 seconds
Most bike repair shops still run on a paper diary and WhatsApp. The phone rings mid-repair, and
the parts get ordered at nine at night. Brindlewick lets customers book online, texts them when
the bike's ready, and orders parts from several distributors in one search. I ran a repair shop in
Leeds for six years before we built it, and 140 shops in the UK and Ireland use it now.

### About 60 seconds
If you've run a bike shop you'll know the phone always rings when your hands are full. Somebody
wants a service, somebody wants to know if their bike's ready, and it all goes in a paper diary.
Then at nine at night you order parts from two distributors who call the same brake pad different
things. I did that for six years in Leeds. Brindlewick is what I wanted then. Customers book
online and get a text when the bike's ready. You quote the job against the booking, and you order
the parts from the same place: the search matches on the manufacturer part number, so you find
the pad whatever it's called. It's built for shops of one to six people. 140 shops in the UK and
Ireland use it, and about 20 in the Netherlands since spring.

## Phrases

### Use
- "Shop", "workshop", "the stand", "the bench": the words mechanics use.
- "Booking", "quote", "the job", "ready to collect".
- "Paper diary": the real alternative, named plainly and without scorn.
- "Made by a mechanic", "someone who's actually run a shop".
- The part's name or number: "brake pad", "gear cable", "the manufacturer part number".
- Numbers we can say in public: 140 shops in the UK and Ireland, about 20 in the Netherlands,
  nine people, six years in Leeds.

### Avoid
- "All-in-one": say what it does instead: bookings, quotes, texts and parts.
- "Seamless": "Nothing in a workshop is seamless" (Maya). Say what happens.
- "Modern bike shops": say "independent bike shops" or "repair shops".
- "Solution", "platform": say "software" or name the feature.
- "Leverage", "cutting-edge", "revolutionise", "disruptive": cut the sentence or say what changed.
- "Journey", "welcome aboard", "thrilled": say what happens next.
- "Workshop management": say what the mechanic does: books, quotes, texts, orders.
- Any number of hours or calls saved: none has been measured.
