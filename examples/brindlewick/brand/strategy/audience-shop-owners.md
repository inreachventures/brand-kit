# Audience: shop owners

## Who they are
The owner-mechanic of an independent bike repair shop with one to six people, in the UK, Ireland
and, since spring 2026, the Netherlands. They buy the software and they use it. Most of the day
they are at the stand with greasy hands; they answer the phone mid-repair, and they do the admin
at nine at night (Maya, intake). Maya was one of them, in Leeds, for six years.

## What they need
- **The job:** keep the workshop full and the bikes moving: take bookings, quote the work, tell
  the customer when the bike is ready, and have the parts in before the bike goes on the stand.
- **What gets in the way:** the phone ringing during a repair; customers who do not know their
  bike is ready; ordering parts from several distributors who name the same part differently
  (Tom's post); admin pushed into the evening.
- **What triggers a search:** [Open: what triggers it. Maya's guess: a busy season when the paper diary
  overflows. Check by asking the next ten shops that sign up what made them look.]
- **What success looks like:** [Open: success in their words. Maya's view: fewer calls during
  repairs, no evening admin. Check with customer interviews.]

## How they find and judge us
- **Where they look and who they ask:** other shops, mostly (Maya). Other routes are not tracked.
- **Evidence they trust:** another mechanic's word. [Open: assumption, based on Maya's experience;
  check by adding "how did you hear about us?" to signup.]
- **What puts them off:** anything that looks like it needs a training day (Maya's guess).
- **Who else has a say:** usually nobody; they are the owner. Sometimes a business partner or a
  second mechanic. [Open: assumption; check with the next shops that sign up.]

## What they are not getting today
- From the paper diary and WhatsApp: customers can book only by phoning or messaging, and every
  "is it ready?" is a call or a text the mechanic answers by hand.
- From ShopLedger: a till system with repairs added on, built for shops that sell more than they
  fix (Maya).
- From SpokeDesk: a US product that, as Maya describes it, sounds slick rather than like a
  workshop.
- From Spannerlog Classic: desktop software that looks dated.
- Nobody in the set talks like someone who has worked at the stand (competitor analysis, gaps).

## What we do not know yet
- [Open: why shops leave or do not sign up.] Maya's guess: changing feels like more admin. Check:
  ask every shop that cancels or stalls in a trial.
- [Open: how long it takes a typical shop to get going.] Check: the support team's onboarding
  records.
- [Open: whether Dutch shops respond to the same message.] Check: ask the 20 Dutch shops how they
  heard of Brindlewick and why they signed up.
- [Open: a best-customer story Brindlewick can tell in public.] Check: Maya to ask two or three
  shops for permission.

## Key insight
They judge software by whether it fits the working day at the stand, and they trust another
mechanic more than any feature list. Talk to them as Maya would across the counter.
