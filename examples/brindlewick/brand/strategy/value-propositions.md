# Value propositions

What each audience gets from Brindlewick, following the chosen positioning in `positioning.md`.

## Shop owners

**Fewer calls at the stand and no admin at nine at night, in software made by someone who ran a
repair shop.**

| Supporting point | Proof |
|------------------|-------|
| Customers book online instead of phoning mid-repair | Product feature. No numbers yet on calls saved. |
| The customer gets a text when the bike is ready, so "is it ready?" stops being a call | Product feature. |
| The quote and the parts order sit with the booking, and the parts search finds the same part across distributors by manufacturer part number | Tom's post on the parts search |
| It was built by a mechanic who ran a shop in Leeds for six years | Maya's history |
| 140 shops in the UK and Ireland use it, and about 20 in the Netherlands | Company records |

### Objections and answers
- **"I haven't got time to set it up":** [Open: an honest setup time from the support team's
  onboarding records.] Until then, say what setup involves, not how fast it is.
- **"My customers just WhatsApp me":** they can keep doing that. Brindlewick takes the bookings
  that would otherwise be a call, and texts them when the bike is ready, so there are fewer
  messages to answer by hand.
- **"What does it cost?":** a monthly subscription per shop. [Open: whether to publish the
  price.]

### What we do not claim
- Any number of hours or calls saved: not measured.
- That it replaces a till. It is not a retail point of sale.
- Named customers or quotes, until shops agree.
